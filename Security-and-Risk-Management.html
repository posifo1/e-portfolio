Security and Risk Management

This e-portfolio documents my learning journey in Security and Risk Management. In this module, we will look at the various areas where security and risk overlap and interact with each other. We will review the various methods of risk assessment, looking at both qualitative and quantitative methods. We will discuss several more traditional risk models (such as STRIDE, DREAD and Attack Trees) as well as reviewing the increasing trend towards hybrid models. We will also review common security standards such as PCI-DSS and look at business continuity and disaster recovery solutions. Each unit includes the module title, learning outcomes, and activities/assignments.

Unit 1: An Introduction to Security and Risk Management

Learning Outcome Reflection

Through this learning activity, I critically examined a range of definitions of Security and Risk Management, developing a comprehensive understanding of how differing interpretations influence organisational policy development, governance, and strategic decision-making. This review enhanced my ability to evaluate theoretical perspectives and apply them to contemporary security challenges.

I explored several core concepts within Security and Risk Management, including assets, threats, vulnerabilities, controls and the interrelationships between them. This analysis deepened my insight into how these elements collectively contribute to the creation and maintenance of an organisation’s security posture and overall risk resilience.

Furthermore, I investigated the Risk Management Process, including the key stages of risk identification, assessment, mitigation, monitoring, and review. This allowed me to appreciate how systematic and evidence-based approaches underpin effective decision-making and ensure alignment with organisational objectives, legal requirements, and industry standards.

As part of the discussion topic, I engaged with the case study by Kovaitė and Stankevičienė (2019), which provided a practical lens through which to apply theoretical knowledge. In this task, I examined the authors’ use of the term Industry 4.0, identifying examples such as automation in manufacturing and the integration of cyber-physical systems. I also identified real-world risks aligning with the authors’ risk categories, such as data breaches in interconnected supply chains and operational disruptions caused by system failures. Additionally, I located and analysed a supporting or contradictory peer-reviewed journal article, which encouraged critical reflection on differing academic perspectives regarding Industry 4.0 and its associated risks.

Overall, this learning experience enhanced my theoretical understanding and critical analysis skills, reinforcing the connection between security and risk management frameworks and their application in real-world, technology-driven contexts.

Unit 3: Introduction to Threat Modelling and Management

Learning Outcomes

In this unit, I explored the concept and practical value of threat modelling as a proactive security tool. I learned how frameworks such as STRIDE and PASTA can systematically identify and categorise potential threats, helping organisations anticipate and prioritise security risks before incidents occur. This reinforced my understanding that effective threat modelling not only strengthens technical defences but also informs strategic decision-making by aligning security measures with real-world attack scenarios.

Activities and Assignments Summary

Through the STRIDE framework exercise, I applied theoretical concepts to a sample web application, identifying common vulnerabilities such as spoofing and information disclosure. The reflection task encouraged comparison between methodologies, highlighting how structured models improve communication between technical and business teams. Finally, the threat model diagram assignment for an online banking system helped me visualise how assets, actors, and data flows interact, solidifying my ability to represent and mitigate threats in a clear, structured way.
